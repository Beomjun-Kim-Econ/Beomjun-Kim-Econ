# Applied Microeconometrics
## (Working in Progress) Post-COVID Labor-Market Outcomes of Mothers in Teleworkable Occupations · [Presentation (KO)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/11mlQwHbHv818amaUIjxCf8PyApgLQVPw/view?usp=drive_link)
An undergraduate empirical economics project examining whether mothers in teleworkable occupations experienced relative labor-market improvements after COVID-19. Using the Korean Labor and Income Panel Study (KLIPS), I construct an individual-level panel of labor-market outcomes from 2016 to 2022 and estimate difference-in-differences and event-study specifications with individual fixed effects, year fixed effects, and 2019 occupation-, industry-, and region-by-year fixed effects. The analysis compares mothers with fathers, childless women, and other relevant comparison groups within teleworkable occupations, focusing on hourly wages, weekly working hours, and weekly labor income. The results suggest relative post-COVID gains for mothers among teleworkable workers who remained employed, especially in weekly labor income, while also showing that part of the observed pattern may be driven by the relative decline of married childless women. The project emphasizes cautious interpretation given limitations such as occupation-level teleworkability measures, endogenous occupational selection, and conditioning on post-COVID employment survival.


# Development Policy
## Can Electric Motorcycles Save Lives? Causal Evidence from Uganda and Rider-Level Barriers to Electric Transition · [Paper](https://drive.google.com/file/d/19zh3mlyXgzdUjU78UCOYUi1JOjuLoN3o/view?usp=sharing)
An undergraduate development economics and policy project on electric motorcycle transition in Kampala, Uganda. Using a mixed-methods design, the study combines a Difference-in-Differences analysis of an Ebola-induced mobility lockdown with field interviews of boda boda riders to estimate the public health gains of electrification and explain why adoption remains limited. The analysis suggests that full boda boda electrification could reduce total ambient PM2.5 by about 18% and avert approximately 510 premature deaths annually in Kampala, while rider interviews show that adoption barriers stem less from technological rejection than from battery-swap unreliability, income uncertainty, policy misalignment, and restrictive financing structures. The project frames EV transition as a development policy problem involving infrastructure, livelihoods, and public health, rather than a purely environmental or technological intervention.   
*Recipient of the Yonsei Social Engagement Fund Undergraduate Excellence Research Award.*
